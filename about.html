History & mission

CSAD was established in 2007 with funding from a “We the People” Challenge Grant awarded by the National Endowment for the Humanities. Housed in Oden Hall, the Center draws on Kenyon’s long tradition of exploring controversial issues through deliberative inquiry and civil, probing conversation.

Nonpartisan by design. CSAD promotes nonpartisan civic and political discourse. It does not endorse candidates, parties, or legislation — its work is to convene dialogue, not to advocate a side.

Leadership

Nancy Powers, Associate Director of CSAD and a member of Kenyon’s Political Science faculty, helps guide the Center’s programming and directs the CSAD Associates program, which involves Kenyon students directly in the Center’s research and events.

CSAD Associates

Much of CSAD’s research and programming is done in collaboration with CSAD Associates — Kenyon students who work alongside faculty on projects examining American democracy in practice, from national politics to local elections.

What CSAD offers

  • Public conferences, lectures, and panel discussions with scholars, politicians, and journalists
  • Teaching and research opportunities for faculty and students
  • The CSAD Associates program for undergraduate research
  • Support for student internships in Washington, D.C.
